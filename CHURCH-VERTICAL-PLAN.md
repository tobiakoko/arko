## 0. Why this pivot works

- Churches buy websites on **trust and referrals**, not cold outreach — a solo founder with an existing church network (DCLM Lewisville, DLBC Tampa) is the ideal shape.
- The competitors are mostly **template farms and lock-in platforms** ($97–$199/mo). The market is starved for custom quality — Tithe.ly's own research pegs professional agency builds at $2,500–$10,000+.
- **85% of people visit a church website before visiting the church** (industry consensus stat). Pastors know this; the pitch is a ministry pitch, not a tech pitch.
- The **Google Ad Grant** gives every qualifying church $10,000/mo in free Google Ads. Almost no church uses it. It's the best door-opener in the niche.
- DFW has thousands of churches, hundreds of church plants, and a very active Nigerian/African diaspora church scene that is referral-dense and under-served by professional web design.
- Nonprofits (missions, charities, ministries) share the same needs and the same grant.

## 4. The professional church website spec (what "reputable" means)

### Page structure (every build)

1. **Home** — Hero with service times + "Plan a Visit" (the #1 CTA), next event, latest sermon, giving link. One goal: get the guest to Sunday.
2. **Plan Your Visit** — The most important page in the niche: what to expect, parking, kids' check-in, what to wear, service length, FAQ for first-timers. This page alone converts.
3. **Sermons** — Video/audio archive + podcast RSS (Apple/Spotify), series thumbnails, search.
4. **Watch / Online** — Livestream embed (YouTube — never self-host video).
5. **Events** — Calendar + registration forms (retreats, baptisms, outreach).
6. **Giving** — Tithe.ly / Planning Center / GiveWP embed; recurring + one-time.
7. **Ministries / Kids / Students** — program pages with photos + signups.
8. **About / Beliefs** — story, what we believe, leadership.
9. **Contact** — map, service times, phone, form.
10. **Optional:** Spanish version, blog, resources, store-free (avoid e-commerce scope).

### Conversion patterns (non-negotiable)

- Sticky header with "Plan a Visit" + "Give" buttons always visible.
- Service times above the fold, on every page's footer.
- Mobile-first: 63%+ of church traffic is mobile (industry stat).
- Max one primary CTA per section. No carousels. No autoplay.
- First-time-guest copy everywhere ("No matter where you've been...").

### Engineering standards (your differentiator)

- **Core Web Vitals green** (Lighthouse ≥ 90 on mobile). Most church sites fail this — show the before/after score in proposals.
- Minimal plugins; video via YouTube embed (no self-hosted media bills).
- **Schema:** `Church` (LocalBusiness type) on home, `Event`, `VideoObject` (sermons), `FAQPage`, `Organization`. Church + local SEO → map-pack presence for "church near me" searches.
- Google Business Profile for the church: service times, photos, posts, Q&A, reviews.
- **Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA):** contrast, focus states, alt text, captioned sermons. Aging congregations + denominational reputation; CPACC cert makes it credible.
- Backups + uptime monitoring included in care plans.

### Giving/sermon/event stack (integrate, don't build)

- **Giving:** Tithe.ly (free tier, transaction fees only), Planning Center Giving, GiveWP (WordPress). Ask what the church already uses — never force a migration.
- **Sermons:** Seriously Simple Podcasting (free) → auto-distribute to Apple/Spotify; YouTube embed for video.
- **Events:** The Events Calendar (free) + registration forms.
- **Email/newsletter:** Brevo or MailerLite free tiers (500–1,000 contacts).
- **ChMS (if asked):** recommend Planning Center / Tithe.ly / ChurchCenter — you integrate, you don't compete.

## 5. Solving the UI/UX problem (your stated #1 pain point)

The root cause: **AI produces amateur design when given no design system and no references.** Amateur tells: no type hierarchy, cramped spacing, default gradients/palettes, pure black/white, centered-everything, mismatched imagery, mobile as an afterthought.

### The system that fixes it (use for every project, including your own site)

**Step 1 — Never start from a blank canvas.** Start from a curated template or component system:
- **Webflow premium church templates** (church category on webflow.com/templates; ~$79–$129 one-time) — highest polish-to-effort ratio.
- **Framer church templates** (framer.com/templates → "church") — same idea, flat ~$15–25/mo per site.
- **WordPress:** Kadence or Blocksy (free) + a church starter template, or a premium church theme (ThemeForest "Church" category).
- **Custom code (your current stack):** Next.js + Tailwind + **shadcn/ui** — but only with tokens + references (below). Rule: *"You can't ask an AI to design from nothing. You CAN ask it to execute a design you defined."*

**Step 2 — Write the design brief in tokens before any AI work** (this becomes your reusable "Design DNA" doc):
- **Typography:** 2 families max. A display serif (e.g., Fraunces, Playfair, Libre Baskerville) + a clean sans (Inter, Public Sans, Figtree). Scale: 4 sizes (H1 ~64px desktop / 40px mobile, H2 40/28, H3 24/20, body 17–18). Line-height 1.1 headings, 1.6 body. Max line length 65ch.
- **Color (60-30-10):** warm neutrals (ivory/stone, e.g., #FAF7F2 base), one deep neutral (#1C1917 charcoal — never pure black), one accent (warm amber #B45309 or deep teal #0F766E). Accent ≤ 10% of the page.
- **Spacing:** 8pt grid. Section padding 96–128px desktop / 64px mobile. Container max-width 1200px.
- **Details:** shadows with low alpha + large blur (e.g., `0 10px 30px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.08)`), 12–16px radii, 200–300ms ease transitions, hover lifts, sticky nav with backdrop blur, consistent icon set (Lucide).
- **Imagery:** one treatment everywhere — warm, real people, consistent grade; full-bleed hero with dark scrim overlay; no clip-art, no stocky stock.

**Step 3 — The reference pipeline.** Before building, collect **3–5 reference sites** that match the target vibe and paste screenshots into your AI prompt with a one-line brief: "Match this typography, spacing, and hierarchy; content is mine." Never let the AI invent a style.

**Step 4 — The screenshot loop (the single highest-leverage habit):** build → screenshot at 1440px and 390px → paste the screenshot back to the AI with a critique ("hero is cramped, padding should be 96px, headline weight too light") → fix → repeat. 3–5 iterations minimum. Professional-looking AI sites are made by this loop, not by better prompts.

### Inspiration galleries (bookmark these)

- **Church-specific:** REACHRIGHT's "Top 23 Best Church Websites" list (reachrightstudios.com/blog/best-church-websites), Tithe.ly's "Best Church Websites" blog, Church Marketing Sucks' annual website awards, and these live benchmarks: **elevationchurch.org, vouschurch.com, marinerschurch.org, transformationchurch.tv, theaustinstone.org, gatewaypeople.com (DFW), htb.org, gasstreet.church**.

- **Practical UI** (practical-ui.com) — free interactive course.

## 8. Selling to churches (process, from proposal to launch)

1. **Discovery call (30 min, video):** attendance, growth goals, what's wrong with the current site, what tools they use (Tithe.ly? Planning Center?), who decides. If a committee will decide, get the decision-maker list before the proposal.
2. **Free health check** delivered in the call (2–3 problems shown, never a full audit dump).
3. **Proposal (1 page + price):** scope, timeline (4–6 weeks), 50% deposit, deliverables, care plan option. Always include a line about Ad Grant eligibility — it raises perceived value of the whole proposal.
4. **Kickoff:** content kit handed over (I provide templates for About, Beliefs, Ministries, FAQ, photo list). **Content is the bottleneck on every church build — never the code.** Collect copy/photos in week 1 or the project stalls. Schedule a photoshoot if they have none (an iPhone + natural light is enough for most churches).
5. **Build in 2 check-in milestones** (structure + content review, then polish), not weekly meetings. Design by committee kills projects — one approver per stage, in writing.
6. **Training + launch:** 45-min recorded training (they have volunteer webmasters), handoff doc (logins in Bitwarden, how to edit sermons/events), then convert to the care plan.
7. **Post-launch:** Google review ask (no incentive), case-study interview, 3 referral asks, seasonal landing page offer (Easter in Jan–Feb, Christmas in Oct–Nov).

## 9. Unknown unknowns (the checklist you asked for — grouped)

### Sales & decision-making
- Churches buy by **committee**; a pastor can say yes, but the board/trustees often sign. Get signatory authority in writing before work.
- **Annual budget cycles** — miss September and you wait a year. Pitch Aug–Nov.
- **Fiscal-year confusion** (church FY ≠ calendar year) — ask when their budget year starts.
- **Church plants** move fast and have no assets — sell them brand+website kits; they're also your highest-referral clients later.
- **Billing entity confusion** — some churches pay via denomination HQ, missions board, or a member's business. Confirm who pays and get a W-9/P.O. in place.
- **Volunteer webmasters** (sometimes the pastor's teenage son) will edit things between your visits. Training + a "what we fixed this month" report builds trust — and sells the care plan.

### Legal, compliance & financial (church-specific)
- **Texas sales tax:** your website services are taxable data processing (per Playbook D's 80/20 model). BUT religious organizations may qualify for exemption — **require a completed Texas Comptroller exemption certificate (Form 01-339, religious organization) from every church client**, or charge tax. Confirm the current form with your CPA. A 501(c)(3) federal letter does **not** automatically equal a Texas sales-tax exemption.
- **Google Ad Grant compliance:** $2 max CPC, 5% min CTR, search-only, no single-word keywords, inactivity suspension, website-quality policy. It's an ongoing management product, not a setup — that's your recurring revenue.
- **Google for Nonprofits enrollment:** church needs 501(c)(3) + TechSoup/Google verification; church must control the account (never put it in your name).
- **Donor data privacy:** giving data lives with the giving platform (Tithe.ly etc.) — never route donation forms through your own stack. Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA) applies to nonprofits processing personal data.
- **CAN-SPAM** applies to church newsletters — every email needs unsubscribe + sender identity.
- **CCLI/Streaming licenses** — livestreaming worship music requires the church's own CCLI Streaming License; not your job to buy, but your job to warn (and a credibility point).
- **ADA note:** religious organizations are exempt from ADA Title III public-accommodation rules, but WCAG accessibility is still a differentiator, a reputation asset, and increasingly expected — your CPACC angle stays valid. Don't sell churches "ADA compliance to avoid lawsuits"; sell "everyone can use your site, including older members."
- **Payment slowness:** churches routinely pay net-30 to net-60 even when you invoice net-15. 50% deposits and monthly care billing keep cash flowing.
- **Copyright:** sermon music, song lyrics (CCLI licenses lyrics), and photos of minors (children's ministry releases — require the church's photo-release policy before publishing kid photos).

### Technical
- **Domain ownership drama:** the domain is often registered to a departed volunteer/agency's email. Verify ownership (whois) before the project starts; transfer to the church's own registrar account.
- **Hosting migrations** from defunct hosts with cPanel-only exports — budget migration time; keep the old host live for 30 days post-launch (email often lives there too).
- **Sermon archives are huge** (years of MP4s). Never self-host video — YouTube/Vimeo embed only. Podcast audio via a podcast host (free tiers).
- **Media storage bills** from old sites (sermon files on GoDaddy storage). Move media off and bill-avoid — instant hero points.
- **Old site email:** office@/pastor@ emails usually live with the old host — port or keep the old plan for email-only after migration.
- **Cache/CDN:** church staff edit pages and don't see changes → always set up page caching + purge button they can use (one of the most common support tickets in the niche).
- **Spanish/second languages** are common in DFW — build a bilingual pattern into the spec, not as an afterthought.
- **"Can you build us an app?"** — answer: yes, via Tithe.ly/Subsplash/ChurchCenter (app + website + giving in one), which you'll configure and design-skin. Never build a custom app.
- **Multi-campus churches** — one site, one sermon feed, multiple location pages with local service times + schema.

### Market & culture
- **Pastor scheduling:** meetings Monday–Thursday, mornings best; Friday is prep day, Saturday is off, **never touch Sunday**. Sunday traffic spikes are when they'll notice the site is slow — schedule care checks before Sunday.
- **Denominational brand rules:** UMC, SBC, Catholic dioceses often have logo/website guidelines — ask; they're usually simple.
- **Design by committee** (see §8) is the #1 project killer in this niche.
- **Some churches will ask for everything free** ("we're a ministry") — hold the line; volunteer work only counts for case-study clients, and charge every care plan.
- **Comparing against free:** Wix/Squarespace/Tithe.ly DIY is "free" to them — your counter is time, quality, sermons/giving/Ad Grant done for them, and "you own it."
- **The "creative" trap:** churches that hire the local creative agency for a "brand refresh" often never launch — your speed and scope discipline are the sell.
- **Easter/Christmas demand spikes** (search traffic 3–5×) — sell seasonal landing pages; have a pre-made template.
