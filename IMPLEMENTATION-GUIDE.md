- **Church networks:** existing church IT/AV relationships and denominational contacts (DLBC Tampa, DCLM Lewisville) as the referral engine for the productized church line.

## Cold outreach: the Loom video-audit workflow

1. **Target selection:** suburb home-service businesses with a weak GBP (few/old reviews, no posts), a slow or non-mobile site, or missing city pages. Build 20–30 targets/week.
2. **The 2-minute Loom:** screen-record their GBP + site. Show **one** critical problem ("your click-to-call is below the fold on mobile — 70%+ of your visitors are on phones") and mention you found two more. Do **not** dump a full audit — curiosity outperforms front-loading.
3. **Subject line rule:** name the specific issue, under 50 characters. "Your Frisco brake-repair page isn't in the map pack" beats "improving your website."
4. **Email body:** embed a GIF thumbnail of the Loom (showing their site), add two timestamps, close with a choice ("Tuesday or Thursday 2pm CT for the other two fixes?").
5. **Cadence:** Day 0 Loom → Day 3 bump → Day 7 new angle → Day 14 breakup. Personalized video outreach typically earns **3–5× the reply rate of plain text**; a solo can produce ~50–100 personalized Looms/day (2–5 min each) — keep volume realistic.

## Free lead magnet — "DFW Local Visibility Scorecard"

One-page scored checklist (GBP completeness, review count/recency, map-pack presence, mobile speed, city pages, schema) delivered as a fillable PDF or form → auto-emailed report. Hook at chambers and CTA in outreach. Mirrors Autoshop Solutions' "listings scanner" — proven audit-as-lead-magnet in the auto niche.

## Door-to-door / walk-in etiquette

Go **mid-morning or mid-afternoon** (avoid open/close rush). Ask for the owner by name, hand over the capabilities one-pager plus a printed mini-scorecard specific to their business, keep it under three minutes, and never pitch on the spot — book a follow-up.

## Referral system & Google-review compliance (keep these SEPARATE)

- **Referral incentives (allowed):** bill credit or flat referral fee for introductions that close — a *business referral*, not a review; fully compliant.
- **Reviews (never incentivize):** Google policy prohibits incentivized reviews. Ask with **no** reward, via a direct GBP review link, timed right after a delivery win. Mixing the two puts client listings at risk — keep referral rewards and review requests in separate asks.

## Realistic conversion benchmarks

Cold personalized Loom outreach converts roughly **1–3% to a booked call**; about **1 in 3–5 qualified calls** closes a starter engagement → **6–10 touches per closed cold client.** Referral and networking leads close far higher (often **1 in 2–3**) in **2–3 touches.** This is why the starter ladder + referral loop matters more than cold volume.

## Texas sales tax per line item

- **Taxable data processing (tax on 80% of the charge, ~8.25% DFW combined rate):** website creation, repair, maintenance, site care, hosting. Tex. Tax Code §151.351 exempts 20%. **Rule 34 TAC §3.330** was adopted and published in the Texas Register **March 28, 2025**, generally effective **April 2, 2025** (implementing SB 153) — keeps the 20% exemption but shifts the burden to the taxpayer to prove any data-processing element is "ancillary" and lacks "separate value."
- **Reviewed case-by-case (removed from the final rule's example list):** SEO, social, lead-gen — **price assuming taxable** to be safe. Risk evidence: Comptroller Hearing No. 117,761 — an engagement spanning custom programming, website design, mobile optimization, API/UX work, SEO consulting, and maintenance was assessed taxable *in its entirety* with the ALJ siding with the Comptroller (DMA's argument that the decision used an outdated "contemporaneous records" standard doesn't change the outcome).
- **Best non-taxable candidates:** stand-alone planning/strategy/consulting, separately stated — supported by a **2024 private letter ruling** distinguishing website blueprinting (goal setting, site mapping, usability consulting, architecture planning, no development) from website development (taxable, template or custom, through launch).
- **Always:** separately state every line item, obtain a Texas sales-tax permit, and confirm treatment with a SALT-aware CPA before the first invoice.

# 8. PLAYBOOK G — LEGITIMIZATION CHECKLIST (Texas, sequenced with costs)

**Week 1 — Entity.** File **Form 205 Certificate of Formation** via SOSDirect: **$300** (+2.7% card fee ≈ $308 total). Online approval typically **1–3 business days**. Name must be distinguishable + include an LLC designator; check availability first. You may serve as your own registered agent.

**Week 1–2 — EIN.** Apply **free** at IRS.gov (instant online). Draft an operating agreement (not filed with the state, but strongly recommended for a single-member LLC).

**Week 2 — Assumed name (DBA).** File an assumed name certificate **only if** operating under a name different from the exact LLC legal name.

**Week 2 — Sales & use tax permit.** Apply **free** via the Texas Comptroller's eSystems (online) or Form AP-201. Have SSN, SOS file number, and NAICS code ready. Allow **2–3 weeks**. No fee (security bond possible only for high-risk applicants).

**Week 2–3 — Bank + bookkeeping.** Dedicated business checking (Mercury, Bluevine, or local Texas bank/credit union). Set up Wave (free) or QuickBooks; keep business and personal money strictly separate to preserve the liability shield.

**Week 3 — Insurance.** Tech E&O (bundles E&O + third-party cyber) + general liability. Insureon medians: **tech E&O ~$68/mo (~$810/yr)**, **GL ~$29/mo**. Ask for **IP/media-liability** coverage in the E&O policy (protects against copyright/trademark claims on delivered work). Sources: Insureon, TechInsurance, Hiscox.

## Ongoing obligations

- **Quarterly estimated taxes** (federal self-employment + income) to the IRS.
- **Franchise tax + Public Information Report (PIR):** due **May 15 annually**. The **2026 no-tax-due threshold is $2,650,000 in annualized revenue** (up from $2,470,000 for 2024–2025) — you'll owe **$0** but **must still file the PIR**, or the entity risks forfeiture plus a **$50 late penalty** and personal liability for officers.
- **S-corp election:** revisit once net profit is consistently **~$70K–$80K+**, where self-employment-tax savings outweigh payroll/admin cost and reasonable-salary requirements. Not before.

## MSA/SOW essential clauses

IP transfer **on final payment only**; limitation of liability (cap at fees paid); **30-day retainer cancellation**; photo/testimonial release; **data-processing sales-tax line-item clause**; scope/change-order; payment terms + late fees; termination; confidentiality.

# 9. PLAYBOOK H — PRE-LAUNCH QUESTION AUDIT

**Runway math worksheet.** Current monthly income $____ ; personal + business fixed expenses $____ ; **MRR replacement threshold = target income ÷ average retainer.** At ~$800 Essentials, replacing $6,000 of income ≈ **7–8 retainers.** Track months of cash runway and update monthly.

**Keep-vs-drop other income triggers.** *Keep* the day income until MRR ≥ 60% of it for **3 consecutive months** AND the pipeline is full. *Drop* when delivery load forces you to turn away qualified leads.

**Business continuity (if sick).** Document all SOPs and client access in Bitwarden; line up one trusted subcontractor with emergency access; set client SLA expectations so a 2–3-day gap is acceptable; keep site care automated (backups, uptime alerts). No single client should exceed ~15% of revenue, so one pause never sinks the business.

**Med spa compliance prep (before that tier activates).** TMB Rule **22 TAC §165.1** bars false/misleading advertising and requires the physician to be identifiable; **before/after photos need separate written advertising-use consent** (general treatment consent is insufficient) and must depict **typical (not cherry-picked) results**; compensated or discounted testimonials must be disclosed (FTC); reviews the practice republishes count as advertising. Put HIPAA-compliant photo-consent forms in place before the first med spa engagement.

**Out-of-scope requests (ads management, full social).** Ready answer: "That's outside your current plan — I can quote it as an add-on or bring in a vetted specialist." Never free-scope-creep; route to the subcontractor bench.

**Price-raise triggers & grandfathering.** Raise standard rates once you hit **8+ retainers** or a case study proves ROI. **Grandfather** existing clients for 6–12 months, then move them up with **60-day notice.** New clients always pay current rates.

**30/60/90-day milestones (operationalizing the 6-month kill criteria):**
- **30 days:** LLC + sales-tax permit + insurance done; own GBP live with 5 reviews; 5 city pages published; 20+ outreach Looms sent; **1 starter-ladder client signed.**
- **60 days:** 2–3 starter clients delivered; first case study drafted; own site in the map pack for 1 suburb; one suburb chamber joined; BNI chapters visited.
- **90 days:** 2 case studies; 3–4 retainers; a repeatable outreach + referral loop running.
- **6-month kill criteria:** 4–5 retainers, **$5–6K MRR**, 2 case studies. **12-month:** ~10 retainers, **$12–15K MRR**, churn **<8%.** If the 6-month numbers aren't hit, pause paid networking spend and reassess the channel mix before scaling.
